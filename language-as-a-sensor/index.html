Abstract

Robots deployed in human-centric environments routinely receive natural-language descriptions of spatial information (“I left my backpack on the table”) that reference parts of the world beyond their perceptual field of view. Traditional metric-semantic mapping ignores this signal, while off-the-shelf multimodal models remain limited in 3D spatial reasoning and are not directly amenable to fusion with other sensor modalities. To convert language observations into a calibrated spatial distribution, we train a Language Sensor Model (LSM) that maps each utterance and its scene-graph context to a multimodal distribution, with mixture weights encoding referential ambiguity (e.g., which table) and component covariances encoding spatial uncertainty (e.g., where on the table the target lies). We then introduce VL-Map (Vision-Language Metric-Semantic Mapping), a probabilistic framework that treats these language predictions as stochastic observations and fuses them with onboard perception within a unified belief map. On the VLA-3D benchmark as well as on a real-world mobile robot, LSM is the only language predictor whose covariance estimates remain within the calibrated regime; fused into VL-Map, it leads to more accurate predictions of the target object location (~70% more probability mass on the true target compared to the strongest foundation-model baseline).

Method

Language Sensor Model

Consider a robot that hears “my backpack is on the table” in a room with two tables. Turning that sentence into a calibrated spatial distribution means representing two intertwined sources of uncertainty: referential ambiguity (which table the speaker means) and, conditioned on a table, spatial ambiguity (where on that table the backpack lies). LSM factors the language observation as a mixture over anchor hypotheses. A large language model enumerates and weights candidate referents from the scene graph, and a geometry-aware Language Spatial Transformer predicts a Gaussian over the target’s 3D position for each hypothesis.

Qualitative LSM outputs

Orbit of an LSM prediction in a multi-room scene. The anchors proposed for an utterance (left tab) each induce a Gaussian component; together these form a multimodal distribution that keeps probability mass on every plausible referent rather than committing to one.

LSM scales to different scene layouts and object arrangements: mixture weights spread across ambiguous referents, while covariances adapt to the extent of each anchor region.

VL-Map: fusing language with streaming perception

VL-Map discretizes the workspace into voxels and maintains a recursive posterior over which voxel holds the target. Language utterances and visual observations are conditionally independent given the map, so the belief factors into a visual observation model and a language observation model: each utterance contributes a spatial likelihood, just as a conventional sensor measurement would. We integrate VL-Map with Hydra, where visual and language log-odds accumulate additively per voxel. The two pipelines run asynchronously: visual updates run at sensor frame rate, and each new utterance triggers a single language update.

Post-hoc rescaling narrows the calibration gap for Scaffolded-LLM, but it transfers poorly to held-out scenes and cannot correct the means.

Closed-loop belief estimation

We plug each language predictor into VL-Map and fuse it with the same prerecorded visual observations, so only the language observation model varies. LSM keeps positive information gain throughout the rollout, while every foundation-model sensor ends below the vision-only baseline: an overconfident Gaussian commits the Bayes update to a confidently wrong region, and the filter cannot recover. Here the robot would have been better off ignoring language entirely.

Real-world deployment on Boston Dynamics Spot

We deploy VL-Map with LSM on a Boston Dynamics Spot across three real-world environments. The prior scene graph comes from an earlier mapping pass rather than ground truth. Fusing the LSM language channel with the streaming Hydra reconstruction attains a mean information gain of +4.34 nats at the target surface and concentrates a mean of 8.7% of the terminal posterior on the target surface. VL-Map places probability mass on the true location well before the robot directly observes it, while the vision-only map has no probability mass there until visual confirmation.
